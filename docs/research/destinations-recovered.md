# Recovered destination entries

These entries were researched and reported but never written to a file — the agents producing them
were killed by a session limit mid-run. They are recorded here in condensed form, keeping every
field that changes a decision: the reason to go, whether it works without a car, the fixed costs
with their sources, the age gates, and the catch.

Fuel throughout: **$5.74–5.78/gal at 30 mpg ≈ $0.193/mile**, round trip. Verified 3 September 2026.

The full field set (`best_months`, `worst_months`, `variable_costs`, `licence_credit_friction`)
is in the agent reports; what is below is what a card needs.

# Mountains and desert — day trips

### big-bear-lake · San Bernardino Mountains · ~110 mi · 2h15
**Why:** ski in February and swim in July, and the only mountain town near LA big enough to fill a
day without a car once you are up there.
**Car-free:** partial, and honestly no. Metrolink to San Bernardino, then Mountain Transit Route 5
"Off the Mountain", **$10 one way, ~1h30 — Monday, Wednesday and Friday only, no weekend service.**
The timetable gives you **87 minutes on the mountain** before the last bus down. In-town Red/Blue/
Gold lines are free.
**Fixed:** Adventure Pass $5/day, $30/yr. BBMR adult day lift **$89–169**. Snow Valley-only $89–109.
Scenic Sky Chair **$25 Mon–Thu, $25–39 Fri–Sun** (Snow Valley $15–19). Sky Chair + lunch $50–64.
Snow Valley's "Valley Value": spend $25 on food or gear, swap the receipt for a free Sky Chair.
**Age:** bars, taproom, dispensaries 21+. Rental 21+, ~$25/day.
**Catch:** car-free this is an 87-minute visit, not a day trip. With a car the catch moves to
winter — chain control on SR-330 collides with a rental agreement that forbids fitting chains, and
the day ends in a U-turn. Go in October.

### lake-arrowhead · San Bernardino Mountains · ~90 mi · 1h45–2h15
**Why:** the closest alpine village to campus, and the only one with a weekday bus that connects to
a train.
**Car-free:** partial, weekdays only. Metrolink to San Bernardino, then Mountain Transit Route 6,
**$7.50, ~55–60 min, Mon–Fri only.** The 18:15 downhill gives a real day, unlike Big Bear. RIM
Routes 2 and 4 on the mountain are free.
**Fixed:** Route 6 $7.50. Metrolink SoCal Day Pass $15 weekday, **$7.50 student**. Adventure Pass $5.
**Catch: the lake is private.** Owned by the Arrowhead Lake Association and restricted to property
owners — you cannot swim in it, launch on it, or walk most of the 14-mile shoreline. The public
gets a lakeside shopping village and a paid boat tour. People drive two hours for a lake day and
get a car park with a view. Go to Big Bear, where the lake is public.

### idyllwild · San Jacinto Mountains · ~115 mi · 2h20–2h45
**Why:** a pine village at 5,400 ft with the best granite in Southern California above it —
Tahquitz and Suicide Rocks, where American free climbing was more or less invented.
**Car-free: no.** RTA's current route list contains no Idyllwild service; the old Hemet–Idyllwild
Route 32 has gone. Mountain Transit serves the San Bernardinos, not the San Jacintos.
**Fixed:** Mount San Jacinto SP $10/vehicle day use, **$5/person wilderness permit** (under-18s
need a parent present or signed consent). Adventure Pass $5. Campground $25 tent / $35 electric.
**Catch:** no bus, no train, one road in from each side, and both close. SR-243 and SR-74 ice,
close for snow, and have been severed by fire. Everything about Idyllwild is lovely and none of it
is reachable without a 21+ driver. Check SR-243 before leaving, and again before leaving town.

### julian · San Diego County mountains · ~140 mi · **2h45–3h15 each way**
**Why:** apple pie in a gold-rush town at 4,200 ft, and the drive in through Santa Ysabel.
**Car-free: no.** No rail, no bus. The Surfliner leaves you 60 miles short in San Diego.
**Fixed:** no admission — it is a town. Costs are fuel, food and the car. Fuel ~$54 return.
**Age:** the grown-up attractions are hard cider and wine tastings, all **21+**.
**Catch:** six to seven hours of driving for a village you can walk in twenty minutes, plus
October apple-season gridlock on a two-lane road, plus a headline attraction under-21s are barred
from. **Say on the card that this is a 3-hour drive, not a 2-hour one.** Only worth it midweek in
late September, leaving campus before 07:00 — and only paired with something else in the county.

### mount-baldy · San Gabriel Mountains · ~55 mi · 1h10–1h40
**Why:** a chairlift to 7,800 ft an hour from campus, and a second chair to 8,600 ft within sight
of the Devil's Backbone and the highest summit in the San Gabriels.
**Car-free: no.** Metrolink reaches Claremont, but that leaves 14 miles and 4,000 vertical feet.
**Fixed:** **Round-trip scenic ride $36.95, one-way $18.47**, fees included, from the resort's own
booking engine. Adventure Pass $5/day at Manker Flats. Winter day lift $63.99–68.99 (third party).
**Lift runs Friday 12:00–19:00, Saturday and Sunday 07:00–19:00 only.**
**Catch:** turn up on a Tuesday and the lift is locked. Worse, the top of the lift puts you a short
stroll from a knife-edge ridge with drops on both sides, and every year people who came for a
scenic ride wander onto it in trainers. **In winter, hiking from the lift is prohibited outright.**

### switzer-falls · Angeles NF · ~28 mi · 45–60 min
**Why:** a creek with running water and shade under alders, forty-five minutes from campus — the
shortest possible escape from an LA summer that still feels like somewhere else.
**Car-free: no.** Metro reaches La Cañada; the picnic area is ten miles up Angeles Crest with no
service and no shoulder.
**Fixed:** Adventure Pass **$5/day or $30/yr**; America the Beautiful substitutes. **This is a
plain Adventure Pass site, not a Big Pines/Mountain High site**, so an Interagency pass works here
even though it would not at Inspiration Point or Vincent Gap.
**Cheapest trip in the set: about $16 in fixed costs and fuel for a full carload.**
**Catch:** most people never reach the falls. The shaded picnic area near the car park is not it —
the falls need a mile-plus walk and a scramble, and the direct approach above them has hurt people.
Groups sit by the creek and leave thinking they saw it. The lot fills by mid-morning at weekends,
and by late summer the creek is dry. Go on a weekday between February and April.

### mount-wilson-observatory · Angeles NF · ~35 mi · 1h–1h15
**Why:** Hubble worked out that the universe is expanding from this mountain, the 100-inch
telescope that did it is still there, and you can walk into its dome for nothing.
**Car-free: no.**
**Fixed:** parking **$5 Adventure Pass**, sold at the Cosmic Cafe at weekends. Grounds, museum,
100-inch gallery and CHARA exhibit **free**. **Weekend docent tour $20 adult, $15 under-12/over-62,
Saturdays and Sundays 11:30 and 13:00, April–November only.** Gate 10:00–17:00, 16:00 in winter.
**Access is via SR-2 and Red Box — not the Mount Wilson Toll Road, closed under the Eaton Fire
order through 31 December 2027.**
**Catch:** the good part is time-gated three ways and most visitors miss all three. **Public Ticket
Nights — the evening telescope sessions, the reason most people want to come — are sold out for
all of 2026 and return spring 2027.** Turn up at 15:00 on a Wednesday in January and you get a
locked gate at the bottom of a snowy road.

### chantry-flat-sturtevant-falls · Angeles NF · ~26 mi · 45–60 min
**Why:** a 50-foot waterfall and a canyon of hand-built 1930s cabins, and it was shut for four
years after the Bobcat Fire, so most people at USC have never been.
**Car-free:** partial and not really. Metro L Line to Arcadia leaves six miles and 2,000 ft of
road with no footway; the rideshare back out of a canyon with poor signal is not dependable.
**Fixed:** Adventure Pass $5/day, $30/yr, Interagency accepted. Day use 06:00–20:00. Total for a
carload about **$15**.
**Catch:** the road is split between the City of Sierra Madre, LA County Public Works and the
Forest Service, and the FS page carries a standing warning that it "may be closed to vehicles for
safety reasons at any time" — independently of whether the picnic area is open. It reopened
October 2024, then the Eaton Fire shut the county segment in January 2025. **Verify the road the
morning you go, not the week before.** The lot also fills before 07:00 at weekends and the
overflow is ticketed and towed.

### vasquez-rocks · Agua Dulce · ~55 mi · 55 min–1h20
**Why:** tilted sandstone slabs that have stood in for every alien planet in television history,
free to walk on, an hour from campus.
**Car-free:** partial on paper, no in practice. **Metrolink's own page says the park is 13 miles
from Vincent Grade/Acton and requires "rideshare or taxi".** No bus. Rail would be $10 weekend,
**$5 student** — excellent value with a $40+ taxi on the end of it, if one comes at all.
**Fixed: free entry, free parking**, sunrise to sunset. Interpretive Center Tue–Sun 08:00–16:00 —
**the only water in the park is there, during business hours**. Total for a carload ~$21.
**Catch:** smaller than the photographs suggest — the famous slabs are ten minutes from the car
park and most people are done in an hour. No shade anywhere. Do not sell the train as a way to get
here; 13 miles by taxi with no bus is a stranding risk, not a plan.

### antelope-valley-poppy-reserve · western Mojave · ~85 mi · 1h30–1h50
**Why:** in a good spring the hills west of Lancaster turn solid orange, and nowhere else in
California concentrates the effect like this.
**Car-free:** partial. Metrolink to Lancaster, **$10 weekend / $5 student**, ~2h10 (derived from
the line's length and average speed, not a timetable). The reserve is **15 miles west with no
verified bus**; a seasonal bloom shuttle has run some years but is unverified for 2026.
**Fixed: $10/vehicle day use** ($9 senior, $5 disabled). Park open sunrise to sunset year-round;
**Visitor Center only 1 March to Mother's Day.** Dogs are not allowed at all.
**Catch: most years are not a superbloom.** State Parks announced a "Limited Wildflower Bloom" for
spring 2025. The window is about three weeks and moves by a fortnight either way; poppies close in
wind and cloud, so a grey day at peak bloom shows nothing. **Never book this in advance.** Check
the **Wildflower Hotline on (661) 724-1180** and the live PoppyCam within 48 hours of going.

### palm-springs · Coachella Valley · ~110 mi · 2h–2h15
**Why:** you ride from desert floor to alpine forest in ten minutes, and in winter stand in snow
looking down at a valley at 70°F.
**Car-free: yes — the best car-free desert trip.** **FlixBus LA–Palm Springs, fastest 2h05, six
departures daily, from $24.98**, boarding at Union Station, West LA Expo/Sepulveda (on the E Line,
free with the U-Pass) or UCLA. SunLine local **$1.00 single, $3.00 day pass**. Amtrak is not usable
— the thrice-weekly Sunset Limited calls around 00:36 at an unstaffed open-air shelter.
**Fixed:** Aerial Tramway adult **$34.95–$36.95 — sources conflict and the operator refuses
server-side fetches, so this is `unverified` and must be checked live.** Ride 'n' Dine $41 from
16:00. Valley Station parking $15 (medium confidence).
**Age:** tramway none. Downtown bars, Agua Caliente Casino and dispensaries all 21+.
**Catch:** the top is a concrete viewing platform and a café, and the trails beyond are short.
People expect a mountain and get a balcony. Also, **SunLine does not run up Tramway Road** — the
hidden cost of the car-free version is $30–45 of rideshare at the end.

### joshua-tree-day · Mojave–Colorado transition · ~145 mi · 2h30–3h
**Why:** the darkest genuinely reachable night sky from LA, and rock piles you can climb with no
equipment or skill.
**Car-free:** partial, and it takes determination. FlixBus to Palm Springs, then **Basin Transit
Route 12, weekday only, $7.00 one way / $11.00 return** — which gets you to the town, not the park.
The village is 5 miles from the West Entrance and the good rock 10–15 miles beyond, with **no
service inside the boundary and no signal to summon a return ride.**
**Fixed:** **$30/vehicle, $25 motorcycle, $15 on foot, 7 days.** Park annual $55. **Joshua Tree is
NOT one of the 11 surcharge parks**, so a foreign student pays the standard $30 — which split four
ways is $7.50 each. **The $250 non-resident America the Beautiful pass is a trap here.**
**Age: none. The best destination in the whole catalogue for a 19-year-old.**
**Catch:** no signal, no water, no fuel, no shops, no way out on foot. The park specifies **four
litres of water per person**. Summer guidance is to hike only before 09:00 and after 17:00.
Geology Tour Road and Berdoo Canyon need high clearance — navigation apps route people onto them.

### salvation-mountain-salton-sea · Imperial County · ~190 mi · **3h30–4h each way**
**Why:** nowhere in California is this strange — a hand-built adobe mountain of scripture, an
off-grid settlement with its own art museum, and a resort sea receding from its own marinas.
**Car-free: no.** Nothing serves Niland or Bombay Beach usably, and no rideshare can be summoned
from the Slabs.
**Fixed:** Salton Sea SRA **$7.00/vehicle** ($5 for 62+). Salvation Mountain free, donation
requested. Slab City and East Jesus free, donation box. **Donations of $10–20 a head are correct
etiquette and belong on the card as a real line.** Fuel ~$75.
**Catch: Slab City is somebody's home, not an attraction**, and the "abandoned" imagery online
trains visitors to behave badly. Around 150 permanent residents. Stay on graded roads, do not
photograph people or homes without asking, visit East Jesus and the library which do welcome
visitors, donate, leave before dark. Secondary: hydrogen sulfide exceeded the state standard for
hundreds of hours in both 2024 and 2025, and on a warm still day the smell drives you back to the
car in ten minutes. This is 8–9 hours of driving in one day and should say so.

### temecula · Riverside County · ~85 mi · 1h30 clear
**Why:** the closest real vineyard country to LA — nearly 50 wineries across 3,500 acres.
**Car-free: no. Temecula has no passenger rail station.** RTA does not serve the wineries, which
are miles east on Rancho California and De Portola. Four wineries car-free means four to six
rideshares, costing more than a designated driver.
**Fixed:** Wilson Creek 6-taste flight $30; **winery tour $40, or $15 for minors 12+ and
non-drinkers**. Europa Village $36 for five tastes, $46 for seven. Two tastings and lunch is a
**$90–130 day per drinking adult** before transport.
**Age — this disqualifies most of the audience, verified winery by winery.** Ponte **21+ only**.
Danza Del Sol **21+ Fridays and Saturdays**. Akash **21+ all day Saturday, including infants**.
Miramonte 21+ after 17:00 Saturdays. They card at the door, not the bar. Under ABC §25665 a minor
may not enter or remain in a bar. **A Saturday in Temecula is largely closed to a 19-year-old.**
Also: California's BAC limit is **0.01% for under-21 drivers** — one taste is an automatic
one-year suspension.
**Catch:** most of this app's users cannot legally do the thing Temecula is for. A 19-year-old
pays $33 of fuel and three hours on the I-15 to watch other people drink. If the group is not
majority 21+, go midweek to Wilson Creek and pay **$15 for the tour as a non-drinker** — the only
version of this day that works. **Bring the passport, not the licence:** some tasting rooms refuse
a non-US driving licence as proof of age.

# Central coast — weekends

**Shared:** Pacific Surfliner is 350 miles San Diego–San Luis Obispo, 8h52 end to end. **From
4 May 2026, six daily round trips extend north of LA — three through to SLO, three terminating at
Goleta.** Student discount 15% for ages 13–25. **Amtrak publishes no segment times or fares
outside its booking engine, and its booking URL has no documented date parameters — so no honest
`{DATE}` deep-link template exists for Amtrak.** FlixBus: LA–Santa Barbara ~2h from **$23.98**,
LA–SLO ~4h from **$43.98**, two departures daily, **both before 09:20 — there is no evening bus,
which rules out a Friday-after-class start.** ReserveCalifornia: six months ahead at 08:00 PST,
**$8.25 booking fee**, and **bookings must be made at least 48 hours before arrival** — a group
deciding on Thursday to camp on Saturday has already missed it.

### santa-barbara-overnight · ~100 mi · 1h50
**Why:** the one place you can reach, sleep in and get around entirely without a car, and the
station is two blocks from the sea.
**Car-free: yes.** Surfliner to SBA, station at 209 State Street. SBMTD **$2.50 single, $6.00 day
pass**. State beaches west of town **$10/vehicle** (El Capitán, Refugio, Gaviota).
**Lodging:** ITH Santa Barbara Surf Hostel from **$56.35**, Social Stays from **$82.00**.
**Age:** the Funk Zone Urban Wine Trail is the town's signature activity and is entirely 21+.
Under-21s get the waterfront, Stearns Wharf, the Courthouse tower and the Mission.
**Catch:** the timetable, not the town. Few services work for a Friday-evening out and a
Sunday-evening back, and both FlixBus departures leave before 09:20. Check the return first.

### ventura · ~70 mi · 1h20
**Why:** the cheapest coastal town on the Surfliner and the only harbour that reaches the Channel
Islands — a destination and a staging post.
**Car-free: yes.** Surfliner stops at Ventura and Oxnard. VCTC Zone 1 **$1.75**, Zone 2 (Coastal
Express to Santa Barbara) **$4.00**. Gold Coast Transit fares unverified — the site serves a
mismatched TLS certificate. **The connection from the station to Ventura Harbor is unverified and
should be checked before relying on it.**
**Catch:** a staging post people mistake for a destination. Without the island crossing booked you
have travelled 70 miles for a pier you could have had at Manhattan Beach.

### channel-islands-camping · off Ventura · 1–1½ hr crossing
**Why:** an hour from a Ventura dock there is an island with no shops, no cars and no lights, and
for $15 a night you can sleep on it.
**Car-free: yes, and it is the better way** — a car sits at the dock for two days.
**Fixed: park entrance $0 — no pass required.** Campsite **$15.00/site/night**, recreation.gov, six
months ahead. Island Packers Santa Cruz camper return **$96.00 adult 13–54, $72.00 child** — all
`medium` confidence, because islandpackers.com returns 403 to non-browser clients (a refusal, not
a dead link). Day trips $63–168 by island. **Fires prohibited on every island; chemical stoves
only.** Santa Rosa runs March–November, San Miguel July–October, **Santa Barbara Island suspended
for dock damage.**
**Age: none. The one trip where being 19 costs you nothing.**
**Catch: water.** Only **Scorpion Canyon on Santa Cruz** has a tap. Anacapa, Prisoners Harbor,
Santa Rosa, San Miguel and Santa Barbara have none — and Anacapa's campground is **157 stairs**
from the landing, Santa Rosa's a mile and a half. People book on price and discover they must
carry two days of drinking water up a ladder. Recreation.gov states you must arrange boat
transport **before** reserving the campsite; students book the site, find the boat full, and the
site is non-refundable.

### ojai-overnight · ~80 mi · 1h35
**Why:** it sits inland of the marine layer, so in May and June it is sunny while the whole coast
is grey. This is the entry that inverts the coastal rule.
**Car-free:** partial. Surfliner to Ventura, then VCTC Zone 1 **$1.75** up the valley — **route
number and weekend frequency unverified**, published only in a PDF map. Ojai Trolley fare
unverified; every fares URL on its site 404s.
**Catch:** the last leg depends on a county bus whose weekend timetable could not be confirmed.
If it does not run on Sunday you are stranded in a town with no train. **This single gap decides
whether Ojai is car-free or not.**

### santa-ynez-wine-country · ~130 mi · 2h15
**Car-free: no.** No rail. The only scheduled link is Clean Air Express — **a weekday commuter
service running the wrong way**, Solvang and Buellton *into* Santa Barbara in the morning and back
out in the evening, Monday to Friday. **$8 cash / $7 Tap-to-Ride** from 1 July 2026. No weekend
service at all.
**Catch:** if anyone in the group is under 21 this is a drive through pretty countryside to sit
outside buildings other people are allowed into. Send them to Solvang or Pismo and mean it.

### solvang · ~135 mi · 2h20
**Why:** the one stop in wine country an under-21 can enjoy at full strength, because the draw is
pastry and a museum rather than a tasting room.
**Fixed:** **Elverhøj Museum $5 adult, $4 senior, under-12 free**, Thu–Mon 11:00–17:00. California
Nature Art Museum, price unverified. OstrichLand USA, 09:00–18:00, gates close 17:45 — **admission
published only as an image and the checkout blocks automated reads, so unverified.**
**Catch:** the 45 minutes it takes to walk the whole of the interesting part. Four blocks of
half-timbering and a museum; people plan two nights and spend the second wondering what to do.

### los-olivos · ~135 mi · 2h20
**Why:** more than two dozen tasting rooms in four walkable blocks — the one valley stop that
needs no designated driver between stops, for those over 21.
**Catch: the worst destination in the catalogue for an under-21.** Its entire proposition is the
density of tasting rooms and there is essentially nothing else in the village. The usual
alternative — olive oil tasting on Grand Avenue — could not be verified to a named business.
**Do not build a plan for an under-21 around Los Olivos.** Route them to Solvang, ten minutes away,
where at least the museum has a verified price.

### pismo-beach · ~180 mi · 3h
**Why:** the cheapest state-beach camping on the central coast, at **$5 a car**, with monarchs in
the eucalyptus grove through the winter.
**Car-free:** partial. The Surfliner stops at **Grover Beach**, the next town, on the three daily
trips through to SLO. SLO RTA South County **$1.50**.
**Fixed:** **$5.00/vehicle day use — the cheapest on the coast.** Campsite $20–58. RV dump $10.
**Catch:** booking. North Beach is tent-only, Oceano takes RVs, reservations open six months ahead
and close 48 hours before arrival. **Oceano Dunes is the one place on this coast where vehicles
drive on sand and 4WD genuinely matters** — permit rules unverified.

### san-luis-obispo · ~190 mi · 3h15
**Why:** the only town north of Santa Barbara with a hostel, a train station and a bus network
that reaches the coast — the base for everything up here.
**Car-free: yes.** Three Surfliner round trips daily since 4 May 2026, plus FlixBus from $43.98
and the Coast Starlight. RTA **$2.00 single, $5.50 Regional Day Pass**; Route 12 to Morro Bay,
Route 9 to Paso Robles, Route 15 up the coast.
**Lodging:** Hostel Obispo, 1617 Santa Rosa St — **rate unverified, the site publishes none.**
**Catch: the hostel closes 11:00–16:30** and reception only opens at 16:30. A morning arrival means
carrying your bag round town all afternoon; a late train leaves you outside a locked door.

### morro-bay · ~205 mi · 3h30
**Why:** a 576-foot volcanic plug in the harbour mouth, reachable on a $2 bus from SLO.
**Car-free: yes, via SLO.** RTA Route 12 **$2.00**; Morro Bay Transit **$1.50**.
**Fixed:** Morro Bay SP **$8.00/vehicle** day use; campsite $20–58.
**Catch:** more than 140 sites and still a lottery for the good ones, with a hard cap of **8 people
and 2 vehicles per site**. A group of ten cannot legally share one and must win two bookings.

### paso-robles · ~210 mi · 3h30
**Why:** **Firestone Walker runs brewery tours open to anyone over 12** — the one wine town with a
verified alternative for an under-21. The tour is open; the tasting at the end is not.
**Car-free:** partial. No direct rail — the Surfliner terminates 30 miles south at SLO. RTA Route 9
**$2.00**, weekend frequency unverified. Once in Paso the wineries are spread over miles of country
road with no transit at all.
**Also age-neutral:** Sensorio's Field of Light, Franklin Hot Springs, River Oaks Hot Springs, the
Pioneer Museum, Estrella Warbirds — **all prices unverified**, Sensorio returns 403.
**Catch:** the distances. This is not a walkable wine town like Los Olivos; without a car and a
21+ driver you will see the downtown square and nothing else.

### cambria · ~230 mi · 4h
**Why:** the only base near Hearst Castle a weekend bus actually reaches, and the elephant seal
rookery up the road costs nothing.
**Car-free:** partial and better than it looks. Surfliner or FlixBus to SLO, RTA Route 12 to Morro
Bay, then **Route 15 — Morro Bay–Cayucos–Cambria–San Simeon, seven days a week**, five trips
weekdays and Saturdays, **three on Sundays**, $2.00 or the $5.50 day pass. Three legs, every one
verified and running at weekends.
**Fixed:** **Piedras Blancas elephant seal rookery free, no reservation, year round.**
**Catch:** three legs means three chances to go wrong, and Sunday is the thin day. Miss the last
connection south and there is no train, no late bus and no cheap bed.

### hearst-castle-san-simeon · ~235 mi · 4h
**Why:** a $2 weekend bus drops you at the visitor centre of a hilltop estate most people assume
needs a car and a hire fee to reach.
**Car-free:** partial, and specifically good at weekends. **SLO RTA Route 15 includes the Hearst
Castle Visitor Center on Saturdays and Sundays** — a genuinely unusual piece of rural transit and
the most useful fact in this entry. **On weekdays Route 15 runs but does not include the stop.**
**Fixed:** Grand Rooms / Accessibly Designed / Upstairs Suites / Cottages & Kitchen **$35.00 adult,
$18.00 child 5–12**. Designing the Dream **$40.00 / $23.00**. Julia Morgan **$110.00 flat**. All
include a **$5.50 non-refundable registration fee**. Booking opens **60 days ahead**.
**Age: none.** One of the few marquee central-coast attractions that treats a 19-year-old exactly
like a 40-year-old.
**Catch:** the 60-day window sounds generous and is not — popular Saturday slots go quickly and
the registration fee is non-refundable per ticket. **Book the tour first and build the transport
around the time slot, not the reverse.**

# Baja California Sur — flight-required

**Shared:** drinking age **18**. The TIJ-via-CBX route saves $150–250 a head against LAX nonstops
and its advantage grows at short notice, because TIJ fares barely move while LAX fares double.
CBX bridge **$51.99 return regular / $62.99 high season** booked 2+ days ahead ($37–42 walk-up).
Ground LA→San Ysidro $21–50. **Baja California (norte), which CBX runs through, is a State
Department Level 3 state; Baja California Sur is Level 2** — secondary sources only, travel.state.gov
returns 403, so the app should link rather than assert. Rentals: compulsory Mexican third-party
liability that no foreign policy satisfies, turning an advertised $1–5/day into **$40–70/day**
(structure well documented, exact tariff unverified), plus a credit card in the driver's name and
minimum ages commonly 21 or 25.

### los-cabos · 2h32 from LAX, 2h09–2h20 from TIJ
**Why:** the only place a two-hour flight from Tijuana puts you in warm water with humpbacks in
February — and the only Mexican beach town where an 18-year-old can legally order a beer and then
be quoted $180 for a taxi to do it.
**Fares:** LAX–SJD return **$342–570**; TIJ–SJD return **$124–234** (Volaris from 275 MXN one-way,
14 flights/week; Viva 7). All-in via CBX **$250–400** against $342–570 direct.
**Fixed:** SJD → Cabo San Lucas **Águila bus $2–15, every 30 min, ~1h10**. **Taxi $140–180.**
Whale watching $90–180. Whale shark day trip to La Paz $220–280. Cabo Pulmo without a car is
**$121–157 each way**.
**Uber:** legal throughout Los Cabos but **prohibited from picking up at SJD airport** — GAP holds
exclusive taxi-union contracts upheld by the Supreme Court in 2026, and drivers face $2,500 fines.
Drop-off at departures is fine. On arrival, walk off airport property towards the highway near the
OXXO and request there. Downtown trips: **~$30 by taxi, $12–15 by Uber.**
**Catch:** you land at SJD and the taxi to your hotel costs more than your flight. $140–180 for 27
miles, quoted at a curb where the alternative is invisible. Everyone who has not been warned pays
it once. Also **mid-March to mid-April spring break raises prices 40–60%**, and **September is
the hurricane month** — official Eastern Pacific season 15 May–30 Nov, peak late August, Baja
Sur's damaging storms in September–October.

### la-paz · 1h57 from TIJ
**Why:** between November and April you can get in the water with the largest fish on earth for the
price of a night out in Cabo, and the town charges Mexican prices rather than resort ones.
**Fares:** TIJ–LAP **$118–197 return**, 21 flights/week. LAX–LAP $175–584, Alaska nonstop 4 days.
**Car-free: yes — the best of the four.** Playa Bus to **Balandra MXN 70**, every 45 min to 19:00.
Águila to Cabo San Lucas 2h45 hourly, Todos Santos 1h35 hourly, Loreto 5h20.
**Fixed:** **Balandra conservation wristband MXN 120 (~$6.50)** via the CONANP portal — resellers
charge roughly double. **Balandra caps at 450 visitors per shift**, two shifts (08:00–12:00,
13:00–17:00), access closes when full. Espíritu Santo bracelet MXN 150/day; full-day tour
$100–150. Whale shark tour $180–230 small group.
**Seasons:** whale sharks **December–March** for reliability; Los Islotes sea lion swimming
**mid-October to end of May**, closed for breeding in summer; humpbacks January–March. Grey whales
are **not** in La Paz — they are at Magdalena Bay, Ojo de Liebre and San Ignacio, January–March.
**Catch: the whale shark season is conditional, not calendrical.** Since the 2024–26 rule changes
it opens only after **14 sightings over 2–3 consecutive days** and is cancelled mid-season if the
count drops below 7. The 2025/26 season opened **mid-November**, not October. Max 14 boats in the
polygon per shift, 3 hours each, GPS-tracked; 5 tourists and 1 guide in the water at a time;
snorkel only, 3 m from head and body, 4 m from the tail, no touching. **A trip sold on "swim with
whale sharks" in early season can deliver a boat ride and nothing else.** And Balandra sells out —
book the wristband online rather than turning up.

### todos-santos · weekend add-on, no airport
**Why:** 75 minutes by public bus from Cabo San Lucas and the opposite of Cabo — which is exactly
what you want on day three, and exactly why it should never be the whole trip.
**Car-free: yes, easily — the strongest car-free case of the four.** Águila/Fradisa/ABC from Cabo
San Lucas **hourly, 1h15, $9–21**; from La Paz hourly, 1h35, $4–26. **Taxi from Cabo $200–250.**
**Catch:** you arrive without a car and discover the town is not where the beach is. Todos Santos
proper sits ~2 km inland on a bluff; the surf breaks are several kilometres along the highway, and
**the town beaches have strong currents and are not safe for swimming.** Without a car that is a
taxi each way on a short taxi supply. Note the Pacific side's best surf comes on **summer south
swells** — the inverse of the tourist calendar, in the months of worst heat and hurricane risk.

### loreto · 2h15 nonstop from LAX
**Why:** Alaska flies LAX–LTO nonstop and lands you in a 1697 mission town on a marine park with
almost nobody in it — the one place here that has not been optimised for visitors.
**Fares:** LAX–LTO **$368–537 return**, ~$300 in January, **Alaska 5 flights/week**. TIJ–LTO
**$122–255 return**, **Volaris 5 flights/week**. Águila from La Paz 5h20, $30–70.
**Car-free:** partial. The centre, mission and malecón are walkable; **everything else is not.**
Isla Coronado and Danzante are booked boat trips; San Javier mission is 22 miles up a mountain
road with no bus.
**Fixed:** CONANP lists **PN Bahía de Loreto** as a bracelet area — **amount unverified**. Annual
CONANP Conservation Passport MXN 3,760.
**Catch:** you go for the isolation and then discover it applies to you. Five flights a week each
from LAX and TIJ, a marine park enterable only by booked boat, and a town that empties outside
December–April. Without a car and without pre-booked boats, Loreto is a pleasant plaza, a 1697
church and a malecón — and then it is Tuesday. **It rewards a planned trip and punishes an
improvised one more than anywhere else in the catalogue.** Blue whale season months unverified —
visitloreto.com returned 503.

# Flight-required — US and Canada

### oahu / maui / big-island-volcanoes / kauai
**Oahu — car-free: yes.** **Skyline rail now reaches the airport** (Lelepaua Daniel K. Inouye
International Airport Station). It does **not** reach Waikīkī — that needs a transfer to TheBus W
Line, downtown the A Line. Fares shared between Skyline and TheBus, figures unverified.
**Fixed:** Hanauma Bay **$25/person non-resident + $3 parking**, **closed Mondays and Tuesdays**,
reservations open **48 hours ahead at 07:00** and sell out in minutes. Pearl Harbor **free entry,
$7/day parking**. USS Arizona reservation fee and window **unverified** — do not publish the
commonly-cited "$1, eight weeks ahead" without checking. Diamond Head **unverified**, DLNR
returns 403.
**Maui — car-free: no.** Haleakalā **$30/vehicle but only 3 days, not 7**; $15/person; **sunrise
reservation $1.00 per vehicle for 03:00–07:00 entry, bookable 60 days ahead** and gone instantly.
**Catch:** people do the Road to Hāna the same day as a 03:00 summit start. They should not.
**Big Island — car-free: no.** Hawai'i Volcanoes **$30/vehicle, $25 motorcycle, $15/person**,
7 days. **The park takes no cash — card only**, as do Haleakalā and Olympic.
**Hawai'i Tri-Park Annual Pass $55** covers Volcanoes, Haleakalā and Pu'uhonua o Hōnaunau.
**Kauai — car-free: partial.** **Hā'ena State Park runs a capped advance reservation system** and
is the gateway to the Kalalau Trail; without one you do not walk it. Fees unverified.
**Inter-island:** hops are 30 minutes. **Catch:** two islands in five nights sounds efficient and
is not — a second under-25 rental surcharge, a second set of airport transfers, and half a day
gone. Three nights on one island beats two plus two.

### yellowstone-grand-teton
**Car-free: no, and not negotiable.** No transit; the distances between geyser basins are highway
distances. Jackson's START Bus covers the town and Teton Village only.
**Fixed:** Yellowstone **$35/vehicle, $20/person**; Grand Teton the same. **Both levy the $100
non-resident surcharge.** No vehicle reservation required.
**Catch: the most expensive trip in the catalogue for a foreign national.** A solo student pays
**$200 in surcharges plus $70 in entrance fees** — $270 before a single night's accommodation.
Four sharing one $250 non-resident pass pay $250 rather than $800. **Put that arithmetic on the
card.** Also: **November–April most interior roads are closed to wheeled vehicles**; access is
oversnow-only by snowcoach or snowmobile, which is a different and far more expensive trip.

### glacier-np
**Car-free: partial, and better than most parks.** **Amtrak's Empire Builder stops at West Glacier,
East Glacier Park and Whitefish**, so a genuinely car-free arrival works.
**Fixed:** **vehicle reservations are gone for 2026** — replaced by a **ticketed shuttle and a
three-hour parking limit at Logan Pass**. Shuttle price and entrance fee **unverified**. **$100
non-resident surcharge applies.**
**Catch:** the lottery is gone, but Logan Pass is now a timed stop rather than a place to linger,
and if shuttle tickets sell out the three-hour limit is the only way up. Full Going-to-the-Sun
Road is reliably open **July to mid-September** only.

### denver-rocky-mountain
**Car-free: partial.** RTD **A Line** runs DEN to Union Station and Denver is walkable. **RMNP is
the hard part** — reaching Estes Park without a car is awkward.
**Fixed:** timed-entry permit **$2 processing**, in force **22 May – 18 October 2026**. Advance
permits release **monthly on the 1st at 08:00 MDT**; last chance is the **19:00 MDT release the
night before**. **$100 non-resident surcharge applies.**
**Catch: there are two different permits and picking the wrong one wastes the day.** Plain Timed
Entry (09:00–14:00) does **not** include the Bear Lake Road corridor — where Bear Lake, Sprague
Lake and Glacier Gorge are, which is where first-time visitors are actually going. That needs
Timed Entry + Bear Lake Road (05:00–18:00). Trail Ridge Road closes with snow October–late May.

### olympic-rainier
**Car-free: no** for the parks; Seattle itself is fully car-free via the Link 1 Line from SEA.
**Fixed:** Olympic **$30/vehicle, $25 motorcycle, $15/person, 7 days, $55 annual**. Rainier
entrance and **2026 timed entry unverified — the NPS page reads "currently being worked on"**.
**Catch:** **neither park levies the $100 surcharge**, which makes this the best-value national
park trip in the catalogue for a foreign national — Olympic at $30 a vehicle against Yellowstone
at $135 a head. The offsetting risk is that NPS has not published Rainier's 2026 rule. Do not
assume last year's.

### alaska
**Car-free: partial, and better than its reputation.** Alaska Railroad links Anchorage, Denali and
Fairbanks, and the Coastal Classic runs Anchorage–Seward; Park Connection and Seward Bus Line
motorcoaches cover the same corridors more cheaply. **Juneau has no road in at all.**
**Fixed:** Denali **$15/person**, and the **$45 Denali Annual Pass covers up to four adults** —
cheaper than four single entries. **Kenai Fjords has no entrance fee**; Exit Glacier campground
free; public use cabins $75/night ($50 winter Willow).
**Catch: the Denali Park Road is still severed at Mile 43 by the Pretty Rocks landslide.** In 2026
the buses go no further: **East Fork (Mile 43) $33.50, 4.5 hours** is all that operates. **Toklat,
Eielson, Wonder Lake and Kantishna are not offered.** Eielson is where the classic Denali view is
and Wonder Lake where the reflection photograph comes from — **neither is reachable.** A student
who books on the strength of those images gets a 4.5-hour round trip. Say it on the card. Also:
many Alaska rental agreements **exclude gravel roads**, voiding cover on exactly the roads people
rent to drive.

### vancouver-banff
**Why:** the drinking age drops to 18 or 19 the moment you land, and the mountains are the best on
the continent.
**Fixed:** **Canadian eTA CAD $7**, valid up to five years, for visa-exempt nationals **flying**.
Arriving by land or sea needs only a passport. Parks Canada fees, Discovery Pass and the Moraine
Lake shuttle **all unverified**; **Moraine Lake is closed to private vehicles**.
**Catch — both immigration rather than travel.** **eTA-X is air-only:** citizens of 17 otherwise
visa-required countries (Antigua and Barbuda, Argentina, Brazil, Costa Rica, Indonesia, Malaysia,
Mexico, Morocco, Panama, Philippines, St Kitts and Nevis, St Lucia, St Vincent and the Grenadines,
Seychelles, Thailand, Trinidad and Tobago, Uruguay) can use the CAD $7 eTA instead of a visitor
visa **if they hold a valid US non-immigrant visa** — which every F-1 student does — but **must
fly**. A Brazilian student who books a cheap Bellingham–Vancouver bus has invalidated their entry
document. Second: automatic revalidation is void if they apply for a new US visa while in Canada.
Jasper's post-2024-wildfire townsite status is unverified.

### puerto-rico vs us-virgin-islands — the distinction that matters most
**Puerto Rico** is a US territory **inside** the US immigration zone: a flight from the mainland is
domestic, with **no CBP inspection**. If confirmed, this is the one warm-water destination an F-1
student with an **expired visa stamp** can visit with no re-entry risk — a genuinely
differentiating feature. **The USVI is a US territory but sits outside the US customs zone**, so
returning **does** involve a CBP inspection. **Both unverified — cbp.gov was unreachable.** A
student who reads "US territory" and assumes the Puerto Rico rule applies has been misled by us,
and the failure mode is being unable to return to their degree. Verify before shipping either.
**Drinking age 18 in both** (unverified at source). USVI: **Virgin Islands NP has no entrance
fee**; Trunk Bay **$5/person 16+ per day**; no surcharge. **The USVI drives on the left** — a
genuine hazard after a semester driving on the right. Hurricane season 1 June–30 November, peak
mid-August to mid-October.

### miami-keys-everglades
**Car-free:** partial in Miami (Metrorail, free Metromover); **no** for the Keys and Everglades.
**Fixed:** Everglades **$35/vehicle, $30 motorcycle, $20/person, 7 days, $70 annual**, **plus the
$100 non-resident surcharge** — and the Everglades page states the $70 annual pass does **not**
waive it.
**Age:** South Beach is overwhelmingly 21+.
**Catch:** a half-day airboat detour becomes a **$120 line item** per foreign student. Either
commit to the $250 non-resident pass across a wider trip or skip the park and do the Keys.
